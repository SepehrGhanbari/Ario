## Experimental contract

- A fresh disposable v0.7 ledger uses synthetic authorization, Floor and time-witness keys. Before the PRE callback returns, reserve-0001.json already exists after the unchanged v0.7 file/directory fsync path. R1 preimage consists of those exact reservation bytes and public pins. R1 is submitted to Sigsum TEST under one ephemeral publisher key; the official v0.14.1 verifier must verify its proof against sigsum-test1-2025. The outer envelope is published via the original R22 relay client; a new A15 preimage adapter uses the original R22 endpoints, connection and event-ID primitive, validates Schnorr event signatures and exact content hash. The callback fails closed if the external commit does not complete.
- The v0.7 inner event and binding are then produced by its unchanged synthetic path. At the POST callback the exact stage bytes are read. R2 contains these bytes and the full R1 proof, plus a new Floor-signed outer binding over R1 checksum, SHA-256 of R1 proof, reservation hash and stage hash. The real R2 proof must verify and have strictly later leaf index/tree size. Only then does the existing synthetic POST callback return. After v0.7 verifies POST and writes final, R3 contains exact final evidence and R2 proof/checksum. R3 must itself be externally committed before the overall experiment returns success; failure after local final is NOT external success and does not erase the local final record.
- The three envelope counts are **phase ordinals**, not R22 memory record counts. All use the original R22 public synthetic envelope schema, same fresh scope and publisher, predecessor double SHA-256 links, and payload SHA-256 in the legacy `floor_commitment_sha256` field (here explicitly an A15 phase payload digest, NOT R22 floor_record semantics).
- Separate publish, discover and reveal runners. Discover receives only first envelope, proof, public key, bootstrap and first local state. No later checksums, proof, payloads or private keys. It verifies the initial proof, obtains the R22 scanner's cosigned head, consistency and indexed leaf inclusion proofs, and requires exactly three matching leaves in the complete bounded sampled suffix. Scanner is copied into scratch and only expected discovery count 1 -> 2 is changed. Original Go tests still run. For each discovered digest, recover envelope via original R22 client and separate preimage via new adapter; check scope, phase ordinal, chain, content hash, embedded R1/R2 official proofs and their indices. R3 inclusion is checked by original scanner in the same sampled tree; the reveal job verifies the withheld standalone R3 proof and exact byte equality for all three envelopes and preimages. Do not give reveal inputs to the discover job. No later proof known before discovery. Old first-only files are checksummed before/after. Job success requires full pipeline and real log evidence.

## What the experiment can establish if actually successful

- In the sampled witnessed TEST tree, three linked synthetic commitments with increasing verified leaf indices and recoverable exact preimages.
- An outer signature cryptographically dependent on the exact verified R1 receipt, assuming standard signature/hash security. R3 payload incorporates the exact R2 receipt and final POST evidence. These are dependencies of signed bytes, not proof of actual wall-clock time or every local action.
- A fresh runner can discover later commitments without receiving their checksums from the publisher. A retained checkpoint detects stale or conflicting tree heads relative to that checkpoint, if the pin and verifier are trusted. The 64-leaf bounded scan deliberately holds if incomplete; no global latest guarantee.

## Explicit unestablished claims and limitations

Not proof of physical fsync ordering, true time of the two inner Floor signatures, absence of parallel signatures, actual authority/provenance, trusted time (the v0.7 PRE/POST fixture still uses synthetic timestamps), administrator-independent retention, permanent relay availability, global latest, durable nonce exclusivity across publishers, independent key enrollment, full rollback recovery, or actual Ario signer use. This is a fresh runner starting from first-only material, not physical device rollback or proven independent administration; no new custodian ledger. A malicious publisher can create alternative commitments, withhold records, or use other keys; public evidence may reveal conflict only within retained/observed scope. Nostr signatures prove transport-event integrity, not independent custody. R22 TEST policy is not a production trust authority. Artifacts expire after 30 days; test logs/relays may have their own retention. Never publish real data or keys; synthetic public data and public signatures only. Public external submissions may persist. This proposed workflow is not a safe recovery protocol for a real signer. All dispositions stay HOLD; no automatic adoption or trust.
