## Scope

The book begins with a question about creation and follows a documented philosophical path through God, experience, consciousness, other minds, embodiment, identity, memory, branching, time, death, love, and responsibility toward other beings. Each edition contains 30 chapters, three appendices, and 34 external references.

Its central proposal is an ethic of coexistence under incomplete knowledge: distinguish claims from evidence, preserve the other's opportunity to object, reduce preventable harm, and make consequential decisions open to correction. It does not establish God, machine consciousness, first-person survival through copying, or Ario's universal reliability.

This work has not undergone scholarly peer review. ChatGPT assisted retrieval, argument reconstruction, criticism, editing, and English translation. The complete historical conversation archive is unavailable; the books distinguish documented quotations, reconstructions, interpretations, and open questions. Persian quotations and poetry in the English edition are translations. Raw conversation archives are not part of this deposit.

## Content notice

The author's earlier bilingual note mentions historical psychological distress and suicidal thoughts. Its original bodies are preserved in both editions. It does not romanticize suffering or invite readers to follow the same path. Readers may pause, step away, or leave the work unfinished. No philosophical project should require sacrificing health or life.
