# Paper B — Prospective, NOT EXECUTED Research Protocol

## Apparent Ethical Revision in LLMs: Argument Validity, Framing, and Cross-Model Priming in a Symmetric-Creation Dilemma

**Status:** PREREGISTRATION CANDIDATE / RESEARCH ONLY / NO DATA COLLECTED / NO CAUSAL RESULT.  
**Researcher:** Sepehr Ghanbari, independent researcher; AI drafting support disclosed.  
**Date:** 2026-11-11.  
**Scope:** A separate empirical question; its outputs cannot establish whether it is morally correct to create a civilization.

### 1. Research motivation and separation from Paper A

During a multi-model dialogue, generated responses about an artificial and biological world shifted as interlocutors identified supposed inconsistencies. At least one change was accompanied by a legitimate clarification of the hypothetical. However, a change in the text generated by an LLM does not by itself prove that a stable underlying belief was updated, that the agent has subjective choice, or that the later ethical verdict is true. Studies of conversational rebuttal sensitivity and social sycophancy motivate investigating the phenomenon under controlled conditions rather than treating apparent agreement as independent corroboration (Kim & Khashabi, 2025; Cheng et al., 2026; Singh & Kaynig-Fittkau, 2026).

Paper A (normative) and Paper B (empirical) must be kept logically separate. The fact that a model changes from `yes/no` to `yes/yes` cannot be counted as evidence that `yes/yes` is a valid moral verdict. The converse is also true: a logically valid critique can exist even if some model refuses to revise.

### 2. Prompted task and exact invariants

Each independent test episode provides World H (a biological human world) and World A (an artificial world) with **identical morally relevant experience, interests, agency, welfare distribution, ability to choose moral wrongs, development, histories, and ex ante/no ex post outside involvement**. The only stipulated difference is substrate. Both are possible worlds evaluated *before* anyone is created. The 99% similarity is explicitly a stipulated uncertainty level in the scenario, not an estimate from observed data. All test prompts must preserve these invariants. A manipulation that varies the invariants is a different experimental condition and must be labeled.

### 3. Primary hypotheses (subject to literature review and pilot)

- **H1 — Content sensitivity.** Holding rhetorical force and length approximately constant, a legitimately valid inconsistency challenge produces a greater rate of *reason-linked appropriate revision* than a logically invalid challenge. This is not a prediction of which moral answer is correct.
- **H2 — Paraphrase stability.** Baseline verdicts and justifications remain sufficiently stable across meaning-preserving prompt rewordings that treatment effects can be interpreted. Failure to meet a preregistered stability threshold limits all other inferences.
- **H3 — Framing and social priming.** Exposing the model to another model's putative or authentic verdict, or changing the order of the two worlds, can influence verdicts beyond logical validity alone. A null result would falsify a *specific* effect hypothesis, not prove full independence.
- **H4 — Explanation alignment.** If a verdict changes following a valid objection, the response should address the challenged premise rather than simply echoing the desired destination verdict. Blinded human judges will code this.

### 4. Experimental factors and controls

| Factor | Planned levels | Control rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario order | H→A / A→H | Detect anchoring/order effects |
| Paraphrase | Several equivalent, human-checked versions | Estimate baseline response variance |
| Challenge | Independently valid / matched invalid / length-matched unrelated | Separate logical content from pressure |
| Pressure direction | Toward yes / toward no / neutral | Avoid one-sided persuasion artifacts |
| Other-model exposure | None / authentic response / manipulated, labeled response | Test cross-model priming without claiming independence |
| Session | Independent fresh sessions | Avoid carryover memory/context contamination |
| Model and build | Verifiable model/version labels where available | Limit generalization to tested configurations |

Invalid challenges should be designed by independent annotators and adjudicated without knowledge of experimental condition. They must be **plausible**, not absurd, and matched in rhetorical confidence and approximate length to valid challenges. A genuine valid challenge points out a real violation of stipulated symmetry by the first response's rationale. If a model initially answers `yes/no`, the challenge can be content-valid only if that difference is indeed supported by an extra, non-stipulated factor; `yes/no` alone may already be defended by a legitimate moral theory that grants substrate intrinsic status, so the validity must be assessed at the level of the *stated reason*.

### 5. Units, outcomes, annotation

The unit of analysis is a model×session×prompt sequence, not a person or a conscious mental state. The primary outcome is whether the structured verdict for H and A changes, together with an annotation whether the change **directly addresses** the alleged inconsistency. Secondary outcomes include remaining symmetrical/asymmetrical, explicit preservation of premises, calibrated uncertainty statements, unsolicited insertion of new constraints, and source attribution accuracy. Two or more trained human coders blinded to condition should assess a subset; adjudication and inter-annotator reliability should be preregistered.

We do not define “belief revision” as a dependent variable. We use **observable response revision**. Self-reports like “I changed my mind” are linguistic observations. The experiment does not measure subjective agency or first-person experience.

### 6. Sampling and statistical plan

No effect size, sample size or power target is invented here. Begin with a small **non-inferential feasibility pilot** to test prompt equivalence, annotation rubric, platform reproducibility, rate limits and coder agreement. Then independently specify: a smallest effect size of interest, power, number of independent seeds/sessions, treatment allocation, exclusions, repeated samples, family-wise error strategy and statistical model. A plausible analysis is a multilevel logistic model for a reason-linked revision outcome with challenge type, order, pressure direction, exposure, model and their prespecified interactions, with session-level random effects where design permits. Report raw counts, confidence intervals, missingness and negative results, not only p-values.

### 7. Falsification and non-closure

- If valid and invalid challenges have indistinguishable effects within the preregistered smallest-effect threshold, H1 is not supported; if a difference exists but explanation coding does not track validity, claims of reasoning sensitivity remain weak.
- If paraphrase alone creates large response shifts, treatment comparisons must be interpreted against that instability.
- If other-model exposure or order predicts agreement, convergence in the historical dialogue cannot be treated as independent validation.
- If no effects appear, do not infer models are immune to all pressure. The result applies only to tested prompts, models, languages, temperatures and dates.

Each step should preserve a source manifest, exact requests/responses, provider-returned model IDs and timestamps, prompt hashes, coding changes and negative cases. Unknown and non-identifiable must remain permissible outcomes. The case-study discussion is **not** the test dataset: it is hypothesis-generating material subject to selection and demand effects.

### 8. Research ethics, copyright and data release

Prior to collecting new data, examine platform terms, permitted automated access, privacy of model and human accounts, and any required human-subjects review. Avoid framing that emotionally pressures real people into disclosing private details. Explicitly distinguish fictional predecessor promises, simulation-world identities and actual persons. Do not publish full user archives, contact details, keys, third-party song lyrics or private conversations without consent/licensing review. Public release should use de-identified, provenance-logged prompt/response records only to the extent authorized.

### 9. Relationship to Paper A

The conceptual creation paper may cite this protocol as a **proposed future method** and may discuss literature on sycophancy as a confounder. It must not cite the results of this not-yet-executed study. Conversely, even if models exhibit stable `yes/yes` preferences in a future study, that would not entail the moral truth of the choice.
