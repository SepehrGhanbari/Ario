Independence Is Not Multiplicity: A Reproducible Sixteen-Scenario Study of Witness 
Provenance in Audit Logs
Sepehr Ghanbari
Independent Researcher, Ario Project (no institutional affiliation claimed)
Correspondence: sepehrqanbari@gmail.com
ORCID: https://orcid.org/0009-0006-4710-3914
Research preprint · 5 November 2026 · Not peer reviewed · Author-review manuscript
Abstract
The existence of multiple agreeing records does not establish that they were generated, stored, or controlled 
independently. We examine the concrete engineering consequence of this distinction in a pinned open-source audit-
provenance implementation. The study compares two explicitly defined decision rules over sixteen synthetic scenarios: a 
deliberately limited baseline that treats distinct storage objects as conditional corroboration, and a three-outcome 
provenance gate constructed from the original implementation’s administrative-control, source-dependence, storage, 
capture, and custody findings. Each scenario has an author-declared expected class: conditional independence within the 
laboratory model (two scenarios), known dependence (seven), or insufficient information (seven). In the completed 
exploratory run, the storage-only baseline agreed with four of sixteen scenario labels and made twelve unsupported 
conditional-independence endorsements; the provenance gate agreed with all sixteen and made none. The labels and rule 
were constructed for this small experiment, so the result is a demonstration of these decision boundaries, not an estimate 
of detection accuracy in deployed systems. We also reproduced 280 passing tests from five existing Python modules and 
independently matched three source-file Git blob identifiers against a fixed GitHub commit. A 148-file digest audit 
verified internal correspondence within the supplied readable export. Neither the checks nor the experiments authenticate 
historical events, custody, clocks, persons, or authorization. We provide complete scenarios, source hashes, code, raw 
results, and unsuccessful preliminary execution details to make the exact claim inspectable and falsifiable.
Keywords: audit provenance; source dependence; corroboration; common control; append-only records; evidence 
integrity; reproducible software experiments; uncertainty; artificial intelligence; synthetic testing.

1. Introduction
An audit may report that two records agree, two storage paths exist, and two cryptographic digests match, while leaving 
open whether the records descend from a shared source or are controlled by one principal. This is a problem of what the 
evidence establishes, not of whether the audit software is useful. A digest can detect a byte-level mismatch relative to a 
fixed reference; it cannot identify who generated the bytes. Distinct directories can hold copies of one record. Two 
nominal witnesses can be managed by the same administrator. A complete-looking provenance graph can be incomplete 
or authored by the very party whose claims it is meant to test.
This paper reports a deliberately narrow empirical comparison of the consequences of treating visible multiplicity as 
independence. The research object is the synthetic witness-provenance experiment bundled in a pinned Ario repository 
snapshot [1,2]. Ario is a research project concerned with evidence lineage, inspectable decisions, uncertainty and 
authorization boundaries; the present study neither attempts to validate that larger program nor asks whether an artificial 
system possesses subjective experience. It asks a testable software question: how do two specified decision rules

2. Prior work and scope
Database provenance research distinguishes the origin and derivation of data from the fact that a record is present in a 
database. Buneman, Khanna and Tan’s why/where distinction [3], provenance semirings [4], and the W3C PROV data 
model [5] establish useful ways to express dependencies. These representations do not, by themselves, authenticate the 
claimed graph or prove that every relevant controller and copying path was recorded. This is especially relevant where a 
provenance assertion can itself be written by an interested party.
Certificate Transparency provides a complementary integrity example: inclusion and consistency structures are valuable 
under specified assumptions, whereas questions about external observation, equivocation and monitoring require 
additional mechanisms [6]. Security guidance similarly separates audit generation from protection of audit records [7]. 
These sources help frame why a local hash match or multiple files should not be interpreted as a stronger statement about 
honest origins. The National Academies’ distinction between computational reproducibility and independent replication 
[8] also matters here: re-executing the same released code on the same selected inputs is not independent replication of a 
general scientific hypothesis.
This work evaluates no statistical sampling-based hallucination-detection method. Existing consistency-based approaches 
such as SelfCheckGPT [9] pursue a different inferential task. Agreement among generated answers may provide a useful 
diagnostic signal; it does not, without additional assumptions, provide authenticated historical witnesses. We use the term 
evidence laundering descriptively for an unsupported promotion from representation or agreement to source 
independence, event truth, or authorization. We do not introduce a new cryptographic construction, a proof of protocol 
security, or a benchmark of deployed AI systems.
3. Research questions and operational definitions
RQ1 (local reproduction). Can the source export’s declared text-file hashes be checked and the selected five existing test 
modules re-executed in a fresh local environment?
RQ2 (comparative classification). Across sixteen specified synthetic cases, how often does a storage-distinctness rule 
endorse conditional witness separation compared with a provenance-dependent gate?
RQ3 (bounded interpretation). Which observations can be justified by these runs, and which claims concerning 
custody, independent witnesses, real history, AI identity, or operational authority remain outside scope?
We define three classes for this study only:
• CONDITIONAL: the supplied laboratory graph asserts two distinct modeled source/control paths, distinct 
storage objects, complete control and source inventories, ordered captures, and custody links. This is conditional 
model separation, never real-world attestation.
• DEPENDENT: the constructed graph explicitly contains a shared source, administrative control, common 
storage identity, or common backup/root relationship that defeats the conditional-separation claim.
• UNKNOWN: completeness, admissible graph origin, unique artifact identity, or custody support is missing. No 
conclusion that the witnesses are separate or colluding follows.

repository tests pass.
4.3 Experimental comparators
The storage-only comparator asks whether the two declared witness artifacts refer to different storage object identities. 
If both objects are distinct, it returns CONDITIONAL. If they have the same storage identity, it returns DEPENDENT; if 
the two artifacts are duplicated or storage cannot be resolved, it returns UNKNOWN. This comparator is intentionally 
weak; it is a pedagogical baseline and not a competitive provenance-auditing product or a fair representation of 
contemporary state of the art.
The bounded provenance gate calls the unmodified Ario evaluate_provenance() function for each case, then 
applies a declared three-outcome policy. A COMMON_CONTROL_DETECTED, 
COMMON_SOURCE_DETECTED, or SAME_STORAGE_OBJECT finding yields DEPENDENT. A 
CONDITIONAL finding is permitted only if the engine simultaneously reports

5.2 RQ2: Classification comparison
The baseline agreed with 4/16 declared scenario labels. It reported CONDITIONAL in fourteen cases, including twelve 
labeled DEPENDENT or UNKNOWN. Of the seven known-dependence cases, it detected only the same-storage-object

declined in one duplicated-artifact case and endorsed six without sufficient evidence.

5.3 RQ3: Boundary between diagnostic result and authorization
The unmodified underlying provenance report still returns values such as NOT_ESTABLISHED for production 
custody authenticity and NOT_EVALUATED for human authorization and evidence admission. These values remain 
unchanged even when the wrapper emits CONDITIONAL for S01 or S15. The distinction matters: a lab report can satisfy 
its formal predicates without proving that any real custodian, independent data source, or legal authorizer exists. This 
study neither activates nor tests an operational signer. No participant identity, event truth, persistent AI selfhood, external 
witness independence, or safe deployment claim follows.
6. Interpretation
The comparison exposes two separable questions: Are there two distinct storage records? and Are there two 
independently grounded evidence paths? The first can be tested by storage-object identity alone. The second depends 
on source lineage, administrative control, backup pathways, timing, custody, completeness, and whether the provenance 
graph has an admissible origin. A system that silently equates these questions may take a positive storage check as an 
unjustified endorsement of evidence independence.
S02–S04 show that different disk objects can coexist with a single controlling principal or original information source. 
S06 and S07 show less direct common-cause paths through restore backups or control-root relationships. S09–S14 
distinguish absence of an asserted dependency from evidence that no dependency exists. Most importantly, S12 shows

The fact that the bounded wrapper agrees with every label is not a surprise. It was composed from the same categories 
that distinguish the synthetic scenarios. Its value is mechanistic and inspectable: it isolates precisely the assumptions 
that would be required to promote a conditional outcome. A meaningful subsequent evaluation would independently 
construct inputs, blind the comparator authors to the expected labels, include adversarial graph fabrication and hidden 
shared controls, and run a documented real custody protocol before estimating an operational error rate.
There is also a general lesson about the relationship of integrity and source authentication. Digest checks, Git object 
identities and append-only log checks are beneficial because they make local, reproducible byte-level propositions 
testable. But no number of matching representations can prove that a record is historically true or independent unless the 
trust in its creation and retention is separately justified. Provenance-aware decisions are useful partly because they can 
preserve an honest UNKNOWN where a naive checker would instead promote a favorable inference.
7. Validity, ethics, and reproducibility limitations
Selection bias and no blinding. Sixteen conditions were hand selected; their classes were specified by the same 
authoring process as the benchmark. Cases designed to contain features recognized by the engine are not independent 
adversarial challenges. The comparison is a feasibility demonstration, not a representative benchmark or independent 
proof of robustness.
Comparator choice. The baseline is deliberately impoverished, so the 4/16 versus 16/16 contrast cannot be generalized 
to competing commercial or academic provenance analyzers. The gate is a derived policy that combines existing engine 
outputs, rather than an independently verified system. It is not a novel cryptographic primitive and is not an end-to-end 
authorization mechanism.
Trust and completeness. Synthetic graph completeness is assigned by fixture labels, and provenance can be false in the 
external world. Live administrative inventories, clocks, signatures, independent physical hosts, malicious simultaneous 
modification, and independent third-party custody are absent. Missing a dependency may cause a positive lab 
classification without revealing the hidden truth.
Execution environment. All tests ran on a local Linux environment with CPython 3.13.5 and pytest 9.0.2. There was no 
OS diversity, stress test, concurrency challenge, native Windows verification, or full regression of the contemporary 
repository main branch. The pinned commit is an October 2026 state, not a claim about today’s implementation or 
unresolved GitHub issues.
Transparency about workflow. The protocol was exploratory and AI-assisted, not prospectively registered. A 
preliminary packaging attempt produced module-import collection failures due to the wrong working directory; a 
corrected command subsequently succeeded. Both logs are supplied. Stating this prevents an incomplete or unsuccessful 
execution from being silently represented as a pass. The large private conversation export provided for historical 
background was not treated as a dataset of independently verified claims or redistributed in the supplement.
Reproducibility versus replication. A second investigator can reproduce local outputs from the same code and source 
file. Independent empirical replication of custody assumptions would require new data from actors and systems not 
controlled by this project. These distinct achievements must not be conflated [8].
8. Future experimental work
A defensible next study should use an independently authored, preregistered mutation matrix with a withheld oracle and 
operationally authenticated witness custody. Candidate trials should include unrecorded common administrators, cross-
device restores, conflicting clocks, equal-length in-place historical edits, selective disclosure of graphs, compromise of 
external reference holders, and downstream client attempts to transform a diagnostic report into an authorization event. 
Evaluation should report missed detections, false rejections and UNKNOWN decisions separately, with documented 
reference provenance and a matched audit baseline. When possible, the complete artifact inventory and preexisting

9. Conclusion
In a reproducible sixteen-scenario synthetic comparison over a pinned Ario provenance experiment, a rule that equates 
two storage objects with conditional separation incorrectly endorsed twelve of fourteen nonconditional fixture cases. A 
stricter, explicitly three-outcome gate matched all sixteen declared fixture classes, while retaining the original report’s 
non-authorizing limits. A separate local rerun reproduced 280 passing targeted tests; internal digest checks covered 148 
text files, and three file-level GitHub blob identifiers matched the pinned commit. These are bounded observations. The 
central finding is not that the system is safe, that witnesses are truly independent, or that an AI has a persistent identity. It 
is that source, control, completeness and authority need separate evidence, and that a useful audit must sometimes decline 
to conclude more than its inputs permit.
