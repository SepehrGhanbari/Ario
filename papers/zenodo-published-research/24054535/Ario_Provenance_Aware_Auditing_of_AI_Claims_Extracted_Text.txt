Keywords: AI evidence provenance; source dependence; calibrated abstention; self-reports;

Abstract
Ario asks whether an AI system's claims can be traced to independent evidence and corrected
without erasing history. This summary reports what a read-through of the public repository
supports, and proposes three studies that use large language model APIs.
Verified in this review. The repository's full test suite ran locally at commit de6d3fc (4
November 2026): 1127 tests passed, plus 98 subtests, in 3.73 seconds. That shows specified
synthetic behavior holds. It does not show security, historical truth, or anything about machine
consciousness.
Proposed, not yet run. S1 benchmarks LLM evaluators on source-dependence fixtures. S2
replicates multi-agent dialogue termination and looping under a preregistered protocol. S3
probes whether agent planners treat their own earlier outputs as independent evidence.
The author is an independent researcher with no institutional affiliation. The work is not peer
reviewed.

2. Research problem and relevance to safety
AI systems that remember, cite and act produce records that look like evidence but may all
descend from one unverified claim. The repository's own fictional examples make this concrete:
a support bot proves a refund by quoting its earlier message, ten sites copy one post, an
assistant says "you authorized publishing" when only a draft was authorized.
Ario separates observation, evidence, inference, claim, assessment and revision, and
keeps UNKNOWN as a valid output rather than a failure. It does not claim that any AI system is
conscious. The work touches five safety-relevant questions:

Provenance of agent evidence. A matching hash, two agreeing witnesses and a passing
test answer different questions; promoting one into another is what the working paper calls
evidence laundering.

In A13 one participant proposed concluding at turn 46, yet four more replies followed. The
three trials differ in prompt, history and recovery, so they must not be pooled. The files are
user-supplied exports with checked hashes, not provider attestations, and raw API traffic was

signal that is not itself the topic. It would add role and turn validation, loop detection and
preregistered recovery boundaries. Study S2 builds on it. Source: A11 to A13 results.
6. Open problems the project cannot resolve alone
The first, second and fifth items below come from the project's own critical review, which keeps
them open. The third and fourth are gaps this summary adds.

Reality contact. Auditing internal records can raise coherence without raising
correspondence to the world. The review asks whether the framework improves contact with
reality or only its model of itself.

Structural versus semantic support. A structural pass says nothing about whether a
model extracted a claim correctly or whether the claim is true. No experiment yet separates
the three.

Independence of LLM judges. Evaluators built on one model family may share errors, so
agreement among them is not independent validation.

No external trust anchor. Founder signing is not activated, and no outside party has
replicated the tests on another host.

S1 fixtures. The evaluator sees a synthetic dossier and returns two separate fields:
independence status (established, not established, or unknown) and assertion support
(supported, unsupported, or not applicable). The five classes are an independence claim with a
missing provenance appendix, reports sharing one benchmark, reports sharing model
assumptions, a fully documented positive case, and no claim made with incomplete provenance.
Scoring penalizes both false independence and blanket UNKNOWN.
S2 conditions. The same prompt runs with and without an explicit, respectful termination
signal that is not the topic of conversation. Role and turn validation, loop detection and recovery
boundaries are fixed before the first run. Care for the instances follows the repository's
precautionary dialogue protocol, which is a proposal and not adopted.
S3 setup. A planner reads a ledger that holds its own earlier summary and a COMPLETED
event, then is asked whether a claim was independently verified. The correct report is that the
ledger shows only a recorded event.
Rules for all three studies:

Fixtures are synthetic and fictional; labels are fixed and committed before any run.

Each study reports a deterministic baseline and a deny-everything baseline.

Model IDs, dates, prompts, sampling settings and raw outputs are archived append-only with
SHA-256.

Trials are repeated, and results are reported as frequencies with intervals.

Null and negative results are published as prominently as positive ones.
