## 3. Suggested replacement abstract (DRAFT, *not* inserted into the public manuscript)

> Multiple agreeing audit records need not originate from independent evidence sources or independently controlled custodians. We study this distinction with a version-pinned synthetic witness-provenance evaluator in the open-source Ario research project. The original 5 November 2026 paper compared a deliberately weak storage-identity rule to a three-outcome policy that also reads declared source, administrative-control, capture, custody and completeness fields. On sixteen author-constructed cases, these rules matched four and sixteen author-declared labels, respectively. Because the experiment's expected classes were co-designed with the inputs and policy, these counts characterize specified decision boundaries; they do not estimate out-of-sample accuracy, deployed security, or independent witness authenticity. An always-UNKNOWN rule matches seven of the same sixteen labels while offering no positive assurance, illustrating why raw agreement and false positive counts need companion abstention and missed-positive metrics. A later, separately identified 17 November development extension constructs distinct latent laboratory states that emit identical observed provenance reports, including three purpose-built false-completeness examples in which an unreported common source or controller remains invisible to the evaluator. These are input-identifiability counterexamples, not observed real-world compromises. Historical selected tests and Git blob checks provide limited computational reproducibility but neither independent third-party replication nor authenticated custody. This work argues for distinguishing record multiplicity, source roots, control domains, data collection completeness and authorization; it does not claim that the evaluated component proves external independence.

**Important:** This abstract intentionally combines the **historical November 5 paper** with the separately dated **November 17 extension**, so it must only appear in a *new explicitly versioned follow-up/revision* after author review. Do **not** imply this abstract is what the journal currently evaluates.
